Democratizing Connectivity

The Bump app turns every phone into part of a distributed peer-to-peer network

Join the Public Beta Now available on Android in Brazil and Colombia

As simple as turning on your hotspot.

Bump democratizes access to connectivity by letting postpaid users share excess bandwidth with those who are priced out of access, enabling seamless, private phone-to-phone communication.

FAQ

Common questions about sharing and using Internet on Bump.

What does Bump do? expand_more

Bump turns your phone into part of a peer-to-peer network. People with data to spare share their Internet connection with people nearby who need it, and get paid for it. No SIM swaps, no contracts, no special hardware.

How do I share Internet access on Bump? expand_more

Open the app, tap Share, and choose how much data you are willing to give. Bump advertises your phone over Bluetooth so nearby users can find you and connect. Your phone bridges its mobile data or Wi-Fi to them over a Wi-Fi Direct connection, just like a regular hotspot.

How do I use Bump for Internet access? expand_more

Open the app and check the coverage map for sharers around you, along with their signal strength and price. Pick one, tap Connect, and your phone links directly to theirs. You pay with low-cost Bump credits only for the data you actually use. Credits can be purchased with a credit card, with Pix in Brazil, or with PSE in Colombia, starting from R$4 in Brazil and 10,000 COP in Colombia. Unlike most mobile data plans, which expire within a month or less, the credits you buy never expire.

How do I earn money with Bump? expand_more

Share your connection. Every megabyte that flows through your phone earns you reward points, and you decide how much to share and when. Your points build up in the app while you go about your day, and you can convert them back into money using Pix in Brazil or Bre-B in Colombia.

Can other people see my data when I use Bump for Internet access? expand_more

No. All traffic runs through an encrypted VPN tunnel from your phone to the Internet. The sharer only relays scrambled bytes and cannot see what you browse, and you cannot see theirs. Privacy is built into the design, not bolted on.

Where is Bump live now? expand_more

Bump is in public beta on Android in Brazil and Colombia. You can download it from Google Play today. More regions are on the way.
